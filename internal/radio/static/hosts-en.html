Tips for hosts

По-русски · These are tips, not legal advice. The binding rules are the radio rules.

Make a show, not a playlist

Open Radio is a place for hosted radio shows. The host picks the music, talks about it and talks with listeners. That's why people tune in: for your selection, stories, reactions and mood, not for a single song.

A simple show shape: tell the story of a track, play a part of it, react or ask listeners a question, play the next one. It's more fun to listen to, and it gives you clips for social media.

Commenting or playing a short excerpt does not by itself give you the right to someone else's music: copyright exceptions depend on the country and the case. You are responsible for everything that plays on your wave.

If you also stream to another platform

The radio doesn't stream to other platforms itself. To go live in both places you need a program like OBS that captures the sound and sends it to the platform.

Every platform has its own music rules and its own detection: YouTube, VK Video, Rutube, Twitch, TikTok, Instagram. A stream with someone else's music can be muted, stopped or give your channel a strike there, even if it plays fine on the radio. For YouTube see the live streaming rules and the copyright help.

Safe to take outside: your own music; music you have rights to or a licence that allows streaming; talk shows without music. There is no "under 60 seconds is fine" rule: short excerpts get detected too.

Social media — so people find you

The full show lives on the radio. Short videos on Shorts, Reels, TikTok or VK Clips are an invitation to it:

  • an announcement with your wave and time: "Fridays at 9 pm on 93.4";
  • your stories about tracks and your reactions;
  • the best talk moments of the show;
  • the "share" link to your wave.

Don't build your promotion on cut-up clips of other people's songs: they get taken down just like streams.

If a platform detects someone else's music

  1. Find which part triggered it.
  2. Take it off the air or replace it with something you have rights to.
  3. If you do have the rights, dispute it through the platform's own process.

Don't try to fool detection by changing pitch or speed or adding noise. It breaks the platforms' rules and doesn't make the music yours.

If a claim reaches the radio

We don't hide broadcasts from rights holders. On a well-founded complaint we can stop a broadcast and close the host's access to the air, permanently for repeat violations. The address for rights holders is copyright@openline.one; the procedure is in the rules.
